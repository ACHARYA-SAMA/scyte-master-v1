# Baseline critique, round 3 (Round A)

The site as deployed after round 2 (commit `7288d81`), captured on 23 October 2026 from the production build on this machine (Windows 11, Intel UHD integrated graphics, Chrome with ANGLE on Direct3D 11).

The client's verdict sets the scope. The home page and its scroll story work. Product, Studio, Work, Team and Contact have animation but feel empty. This document measures that emptiness, ranks the fifteen weaknesses that do the most damage, and names the set piece in [`set-pieces.md`](set-pieces.md) that fixes each.

## How it was captured

| What | How | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Every route at 390, 1440 and 1920 wide | `scripts/qa-baseline.mjs` steps one viewport at a time down each page, waits for the scene to settle, and takes two frames 600 ms apart | `docs/qa/baseline/*.png` (not committed, regenerated by the script) |
| Density audit | `scripts/qa-density.py` cuts each frame into the brief's 12 × 8 grid. A cell counts if it holds structure (edges), texture or motion (it changed between the two frames). Its **strict** reading asks more: a real subject (at least 3 percent of the cell is edges) or real motion (2 percent of its pixels moved). A hairline, the film grain or a gradient alone does not pass it | Committed: the 1440 contact sheets and the per-route summary in `docs/baseline/` (empty cells hatched red, hairline-only cells amber). Every frame's cells are in `docs/qa/baseline/density.json` |
| Attract-mode check | The same capture with a 4 s settle and a 1 s gap, so the two frames show what a screen does once the visitor stops | `docs/qa/baseline-idle/` |
| Interaction recordings | `scripts/qa-record.mjs` scrolls each route and, on every screen, uses what is there: it holds what says Hold, drags what says Drag or Scrub, and presses buttons. It records from Chrome's screencast, and `scripts/qa-frames-to-video.py` writes a WebM | `docs/qa/baseline/video/*.webm` |
| Placeholders and metadata | The server-rendered HTML of each route, searched for placeholder markers, plus each route's title and Open Graph tags | This document, weakness 3 |

The audit is a floor, not a judge. It cannot tell a meaningful hairline from a decorative one, which is why there are two readings. The ranking below comes from looking at every frame, with the numbers as evidence.

## The fifteen weaknesses, ranked

Ranked by how much each one costs the client's goal: a stranger should assume a top-tier studio with a large budget made this. Each row names the set pieces that fix it (IDs from `set-pieces.md`).

### 1. Only Home opens on an object

Home opens on the glass logo with light running through it, and the visitor knows at once where they are. Every other page opens on type.

- **Product:** a headline and a row of six small station links (frame `product_1440_00`, strict 47 %).
- **Studio:** a long sentence and three links (`studio_1440_00`, strict 47 %).
- **Work:** a title and a strip of thumbnails.
- **Contact:** a headline and an empty chat (`contact_1440_00`, strict 46 %; two-thirds of the phone is blank).
- **Team:** opens on a HUD, but its subject is a placeholder.

The opening screen is where "premium" is decided, and five of six pages spend it on type and white space.

**Fixed by:** P1 The Line, S1 The Triptych, W1 The Tunnel, T1 The Scan (with the dossier skin), K1 Let's Talk.

### 2. Team, Contact and Work are two or three screens of content

Team has two screens before the footer and Contact three. Work has two, then a khaki band and the footer. On Team the shared footer is half the page. A visitor who arrives from the home story finds the destination smaller than the trip. Strict density: Contact 34 %, Case 00 47 %, Work 52 %, Team 60 %.

**Fixed by:** the full sets for these pages: T1 to T8, K1 to K7, W1 to W8, and C1 to C8 for Case 00.

### 3. Placeholders read as unfinished

Counted in the server-rendered HTML:

| Where | What shows publicly |
|---|---|
| `/team` | `[ FILL … ]` **14 times**, `[BRAND NAME]` once in the intro line, and "Four seats, from our plan." |
| `/studio` | `[CONFIRM]` **10 times** (timelines and prices under What you get) |
| `/product` | `[BRAND NAME]` in the page title and Open Graph title, twice in the page; `[ FILL: ₹ A MONTH ]` on all three plans; "Live funnel" on sample data (twice) |
| `/` | `[BRAND NAME]` once, in the hero aside |
| Footer, every page | The LinkedIn link points at a URL containing "undefined" |

Each marker is honest, but a dashed `[ FILL ]` box reads as a site that is not done.

**Fixed by:**
- T1 and T5: the classified-dossier skin with redaction bars and "Clearance pending".
- S10: "Scoped on a call".
- P11: "Pricing with your pilot", behind `SHOW_PRICING`.
- P10: the "Sample data" ribbon, with "Live" renamed "Sample".
- The content fixes in brief section 7: one `PRODUCT_NAME` constant kept out of titles and Open Graph tags, and the LinkedIn URL flagged in config.

### 4. Product's second half is spreadsheets

From frame `product_1440_20` onward, the machine ends as a form:
- economics sliders, a 13-row "every number" table and a 30-row chart-data table;
- a dashboard of flat charts;
- a list of ten playbooks;
- a 14-day list;
- three plan cards.

The first half earns attention with six scenes; the second half spends it. This is where a CFO-minded visitor decides, and it looks like the least designed part of the site.

**Fixed by:** P9 The Control Room (knobs, lever, gauge, waterfall, receipt), P10 The Instrument Cluster, P11 The Cartridge Shelf, P12 The Flight Plan, P13 The Lever. The tables move into the receipt and a "data table" disclosure, so the paper trail stays, accessibly.

### 5. One entrance for everything

`src/components/motion/Reveal.tsx` gives every heading on every page the same masked line rise, and every paragraph the same clip wipe. That is the "identical fade-ups on everything" pattern the brief bans, in a different coat. By the third page it reads as a template.

**Fixed by:** every set piece choreographs its own entrance (`data-motion-own`), from its page's technique. Reveal shrinks to the copy between set pieces, and each page's world gets its own entrance verb: a pour on Home, a mechanical slide on Product, a blueprint draw on Studio, a projector flicker on Work, a scan on Team, a type-on on Contact.

### 6. A subject, but no depth

Most screens are one layer: a headline left, a widget right, the green ground, and the grid floor as the only texture (the amber cells on the contact sheets). There is no foreground layer and no secondary detail: no readouts, no tick marks, no counters. That is why the strict density sits at 47 to 69 percent on pages that look busy at a glance.

**Fixed by:** G1 The Instrument Frame (a foreground layer on every screen: rulers, readouts, a real frame counter, a chapter strip), and the density doctrine applied per set piece. Each needs three depth layers and five detail objects before it passes.

### 7. Small widgets in big rooms

Things that should fill the screen sit small inside it:
- **Send:** the phones are about 45 percent of the viewport height.
- **Studio:** each workbench takes half the width.
- **Economics:** the model sits in one column.
- **Team:** the roster is a text list.
- **Contact:** the form is a narrow block.

The interactions are good; the staging makes them look like embeds.

**Fixed by:** P6 The Phone Wall, S5 to S7 (workbenches at full viewport), P9, T8, K2.

### 8. Every page is the same room

The same dark green void with the same grid floor sits behind Product, Studio, Team and Case 00. Most of the 1440 frames use the same composition: headline on the left, object on the right. Change the headline and the pages are interchangeable. That is the opposite of "uniquely awesome in their own way".

**Fixed by:** the per-page worlds in `set-pieces.md`, each with its own material, verb, sound bed and grade arc. The grid floor stays only where it means something: Studio's blueprint.

### 9. Banned patterns crept in

Round 2 added a soft light that trails the cursor through dark sections, and a glare on tilting cards that follows the cursor across the card. Both are cursor-following spotlights, which the brief bans. The team HUD's tool chips are pill-like tags.

**Fixed by:** removal in Round D (G3 Cursor). The tilt stays without the glare. Tool chips become a typeset list in the dossier (T2, T5). Round E re-audits every screen against the slop list.

### 10. Nothing happens when you stop

Round 2 gave the 3D scenes an idle drift, but the DOM set pieces sit still once the visitor stops: phones, workbenches, the economics model, the dashboard, the roster, the form. The attract-mode pass waited four seconds on every screen, then compared two frames a second apart. A screen counts as near-still when under 3 percent of its cells moved:

| Route | Near-still screens after 4 s |
|---|---|
| `/work/this-site` | 10 of 12 |
| `/product` | 12 of 29 (the whole second half) |
| `/contact` | 3 of 5 |
| `/work` | 2 of 5 |
| `/studio` | 3 of 13 |
| `/` | 2 of 14 |

**Fixed by:** G8 attract mode with one shared idle clock, and an idle behaviour written for every set piece. Phones receive messages, the flap boards flip, the scan targets each seat in turn, and the knobs demo a sweep.

### 11. Work is a gallery of this site

Eight of the eleven tiles are screenshots of other pages of this site, and three are dashed "open slot" cards. The page proves craft only by pointing elsewhere, and the open slots look like missing content.

**Fixed by:**
- W1 The Tunnel: real recordings of the scenes, not stills.
- W2 Blueprint Slots: the empty slots become drawings with a clear action.
- W3 to W7 The Lab: five playable experiments that prove the craft on the page itself.
- C1 to C8: the making-of, which becomes the real case study.

### 12. Controls are browser-grade

Range inputs and checkboxes drive:
- the economics model;
- the holdout size;
- the website bench;
- the social calendar;
- the consent gate.

They are accessible and correct, and they look like a settings page, not an instrument. A machine should be operated with hardware.

**Fixed by:** the Round B library: RotaryKnob, LeverSwitch, ToggleSwitch and HoldToConfirm, all keyboard-operable with detents and sound. Used in P3, P8, P9, P13, S4, S5, W5 and W6.

### 13. Moving between pages has no sense of place

Every navigation plays the same liquid wipe, and sound is a handful of blips, off by default. Going from the factory to the workshop to the dossier room should feel like moving between places.

**Fixed by:**
- G2: six transitions chosen by destination, with the logo, nav and phone persisting as shared elements.
- G4: Foley and a room tone per page.
- G9: a short intro per route tied to real readiness.

### 14. Every page ends the same way, and the ending does nothing

The footer is the same on every route: a call to action, the links, the giant static logo. It runs two to three screens long. The last thing each page says is identical and inert.

**Fixed by:** G10 The Playable Logo (the letters become rigid bodies you can throw, which then settle back into the exact logo), and a per-page finale before the footer: P13 The Lever, K7 Sent, W8 The Door, T7 Open Seat. The footer itself gets shorter.

### 15. Mobile is the desktop shrunk

At 390 wide, Product is 33 screens of headings, paragraphs and small widgets stacked one under the next.
- Phones are shown inside the phone at about half their size.
- Workbench controls sit under a thumbnail of the thing they control.
- The economics model is a column of sliders.

The density reading is high because the screen is full of text; that is the problem. Nothing is designed for a thumb.

**Fixed by:** the mobile treatment written for every set piece. Phones become the screen (P6, S6, K2). The machine line turns vertical (P1). Knobs get large hit areas (P9). Inspectors become bottom sheets (S5). The Instrument Frame becomes a slim top and bottom strip.

## What works, and stays

- **The home story:** the glass logo, the drops, the five chapters, the hold-to-clamp leaks, the interactive phone, the illustrative ledger with its caption. The three home additions (H1 to H3) slot in without touching it.
- **The chat engine** that drives every phone, and the contact form's server action with its no-JS fallback.
- **The plumbing:** the persistent canvas with lazy scenes, shader warm-up and GPU tiering (home holds 60 fps on this integrated GPU since round 2), the loader, the command palette, the motion and performance switches, the metadata system.
- **The truthful copy voice:** "Illustrative", "Sample", "Fictional store, fictional shopper".
- **The logo.** The diff was re-run for this round: worst mismatch 0.250 percent against a 0.5 percent budget, PASS.
- **Archivo**, verified with fontTools: `wght` 100 to 900 and `wdth` 62 to 125 are both in the shipped file. It has no Devanagari or Telugu, so Contact's language switch needs the script-specific fallback the brief allows.

## Research behind the fixes

The reference studios were captured live: scroll captures and library sniffing, with the sheets in `docs/recon/studios/`. Principles taken, never pixels:

| Studio | Principle | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Igloo Inc. | One procedurally varied hero object per section; HUD labels on leader lines anchored to the object; scene changes that are themselves effects; sound synced to motion | P1, T2, G2, G4 |
| Active Theory | Type and the 3D object interleave in depth instead of sitting side by side | S1, K1 |
| Territory Studio | An index list over full-bleed previews that change on hover | S2 |
| Obys | A bracket "lens" focuses one item in a moving strip; list, strip and grid views of the same work | W1, T8 |
| Immersive Garden | Restraint: one sculpted subject and a deliberate pause | One pause per page (T8, K6) |
| Lusion | A loader that counts real progress; framed viewports with corner marks | G9, G1 |
| Basement | A place as the hero, not a picture of a product | P1, K5 |

For the control room and the HUDs, the reference is real instrument practice: NASA's HUD format studies and SAE ARP5288's guidance on symbology and clutter. Every glowing number on an instrument needs a reason, and clutter is a failure, not decoration. That is why the Instrument Frame is hairline and the readouts are real, not invented.

| 2 | The dashboard laptop's screen is about 2.5 screens tall, with an orphan block beside the revenue chart | Client screenshot 1; `docs/render-fixes.md` bug 1 | **P10**: LaptopShell (16:10, inner scroll) and the dashboard app on one grid |
